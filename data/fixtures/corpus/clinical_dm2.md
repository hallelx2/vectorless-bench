# Type 2 Diabetes Management Guideline

This guideline summarises the pharmacologic management and monitoring of adults
with type 2 diabetes mellitus. It is intended for primary care use.

## Pharmacologic Therapy

Pharmacologic therapy should be individualised. Treatment is generally escalated
in a stepwise fashion when glycaemic targets are not met.

### First-Line: Metformin

Metformin is recommended as first-line pharmacologic therapy for most adults with
type 2 diabetes, in the absence of contraindications. The typical starting dose is
500 mg once or twice daily with meals, titrated gradually to reduce gastrointestinal
side effects. Metformin should be reviewed when the estimated glomerular filtration
rate falls below 30 mL/min/1.73m².

### Second-Line: GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

When glycaemic targets are not achieved on first-line therapy, a GLP-1 receptor
agonist is recommended as a second-line option, particularly in patients with
established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or who would benefit from weight
reduction.

### Second-Line: SGLT2 Inhibitors

An SGLT2 inhibitor is an alternative second-line option, preferred in patients with
heart failure or chronic kidney disease.

## Monitoring

Regular monitoring is essential to guide therapy escalation.

### HbA1c Targets

A general HbA1c target of less than 7.0% is recommended for many non-pregnant
adults. Targets should be relaxed for older adults or those with limited life
expectancy or significant comorbidity.

### Renal Monitoring

Renal function should be assessed at least annually, and more frequently in
patients receiving metformin or SGLT2 inhibitors.
